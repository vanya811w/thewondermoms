What I Actually Needed For Postpartum Recovery (That Nobody Told Me About)

A third-degree tear taught me more about recovery than any parenting book. These five products are why I made it through.

Nobody warns you about the walk to the bathroom after a third-degree tear. The nurses were angels — I can't say that enough — but the discharge bag they sent me home with was basically a squirt bottle, a stack of mesh underwear that made me feel like a scarecrow, and a cheerful "you're doing great!" That bag was not a recovery plan.

The first week home, I figured out what I actually needed the hard way, mostly by crying and ordering things at 3 a.m. The hospital covers the clinical stuff — that's their job — but nobody hands you a plan for the first two weeks of sitting down, standing up, feeding, and walking to the bathroom like you're eighty. By week two I had it dialed in, and I honestly believe these five products are why I look back on that month with something close to fondness. Buy them before you give birth, so you're not panic-ordering from a hospital bed like I was.

What Earned a Spot on the Bathroom Shelf

Frida Mom Postpartum Recovery Kit ($49) — Everything in one box: the angled peri bottle (you'll understand why the angle matters on day two), instant ice packs that actually curve where you need them, and mesh underwear that doesn't feel like a crime against humanity. It felt expensive until the first time I used it. After that it felt like the best deal of my life.

Always Discreet Underwear ($15) — Real underwear with real coverage, and they can hold an ice pack. I wore them for three weeks straight and never felt like I was wearing a diaper. Nobody talks about how long postpartum bleeding actually lasts. Nobody.

Lansinoh Nipple Cream ($9) — By day three, breastfeeding felt like glass. This lanolin cream was the only thing that made the cracks bearable, and it's safe for baby, so no wiping it off before feedings. Nine dollars, and I would have paid nine hundred.

Earth Mama Perineal Spray ($12) — Cold, herbal, witch hazel relief in a spray bottle. I kept one on the back of the toilet and one in the bathroom cabinet and used them about eight times a day. It's the difference between dreading the bathroom and mildly tolerating it.

Kindred Bravely Postpartum Underwear ($28) — High-waisted, soft, and they actually look like underwear instead of medical supplies. I ordered these at 2 a.m. from the hospital bed when the mesh stuff had finally broken me. Sizing runs small, so go up a size.

Why We Recommend These

I tested every one of these on my own body during the ugliest first month of my life — no free samples, no brand relationships. These five earned their spot by getting me through the walk to the bathroom, the 3 a.m. feedings, and the first week of breastfeeding. If I had a second baby, I'd buy all five again tomorrow.

What to Look For

  • Bought before your due date. Recovery shopping at 3 a.m. with a newborn is misery. Pack these in your hospital bag now.
  • Fragrance-free and gentle. Everything down there is raw. Fragrance-free only.
  • Extras you can stock up on. Underwear and spray are consumables — grab two of each.
  • Ask your OB. Every recovery is different; this is what worked for a third-degree tear.

🛒 Where to buy: All products available on Amazon. Stock up before your due date — the Frida kit sells out around every holiday, and you don't want to be fighting other mamas for the last one.
