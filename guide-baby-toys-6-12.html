6-12 Months: The Toys That Actually Held My Baby's Attention For More Than 5 Minutes

The day my daughter turned 7 months old, she looked at her baby gym like it had personally offended her. This is the story of how I found the toys she actually played with.

For the first six months, I thought I had this parenting thing figured out. My daughter played happily with her play gym, her rattle, her crinkly firefly. Then she turned seven months, sat up, and looked at her baby gym with the kind of disgust usually reserved for expired yogurt. She never touched it again.

I panicked. Nobody warned me that a baby's toy needs change overnight the moment she starts sitting. Suddenly she wanted to stand, to open, to pull, to bang. She had opinions. She'd crawl to her basket, inspect a toy, and throw it with the casual cruelty of a food critic.

I started buying "big kid" toys at random and watching her ignore all of them. After three weeks and $120 of misses, I sat down and actually watched her play. The answer changed everything: she didn't need more toys. She needed toys that did something back.

Here are the six that finally worked — the ones that held her attention past the five-minute mark. (That was the old record, in case you're wondering how low the bar was.)

VTech Sit-to-Stand Walker

$35, and the only reason I can wash dishes anymore. The VTech sit-to-stand walker is what she pushes around the living room for 20+ minutes at a time, and the front panel is packed with buttons that do things — music, light-up piano keys, a little phone. It converts from sit-to-play to stand-to-walk, so it carried us from month 7 to month 12. Warning: the music is catchy. You'll be humming it in the shower.

Stacking Cups

$6. SIX DOLLARS. These stacking cups took over our entire household. She stacks them, knocks them down, chews them, wears one as a hat, and throws them so I'll pick them up (it's a game now, apparently). There are a dozen cups and they nest into one. I will never understand the appeal, but I will not argue with results. This is the best $6 I've ever spent on anything, including my last cup of coffee.

Fat Brain Toys Dimpl

$10, and it's just a silicone board with bubbles you push in and out. But the Dimpl has the exact right texture and resistance for a 7-month-old, and the pop sound makes her cackle. It goes in the diaper bag, the car, the stroller. When she was teething, I'd hand it to her straight from the fridge — it's that durable.

Melissa & Doug Shape Sorter

The classic, and it works. $12. The Melissa & Doug shape sorter isn't being sorted yet — she mostly bangs the shapes together and eats the triangle — but she will sit with this thing and study it like it's a tax document. At around 9 months she figured out how to open the lid, which we celebrated like a national holiday. The shapes are big enough that I never worried about choking.

Skip Hop Activity Center

$130. This is the splurge, and I debated it for weeks before caving. The Skip Hop activity center is a seated activity station with a ring of toys that rotates around her, and it gave me 30-40 minute stretches of independent play starting around 8 months. Thirty minutes of her not touching me. I didn't know that was legal. She eventually started "walking" in it, which means it earned its price three times over. I found ours on sale for $95 — keep an eye on it.

Munchkin Bath Toys

$8 for the set. Bath time used to be a 6-minute sprint to get her clean before the screaming started. Now it's a 25-minute affair where she pours water from cup to cup and laughs at a tiny whale. The Munchkin bath toys have cups that suction to the tub wall, which you don't know you need until you see a baby discover it. Water pouring is apparently the funniest thing in the world at this age. I don't make the rules.

What I Learned (So You Don't Repeat My $120 Mistake)

At this stage, babies don't want toys you hand them. They want toys they can move, knock over, and drop — toys that react. If a toy doesn't do something when your baby does something to it, they'll be done with it in 90 seconds. Also: don't buy a bunch of stuff at once. My daughter played with the $6 stacking cups before anything else, and I only bought those as a "whatever" add-on to hit free shipping.

Six months in, my living room looks like a toddler garage sale, but my daughter plays independently for stretches I couldn't have dreamed of at month six. That's worth every penny.

🛒 Where to buy: All six favorites — the VTech walker, stacking cups, Dimpl, shape sorter, activity center, and bath toys — are on Amazon via the links above. Everything except the activity center is under $40, and the activity center goes on sale regularly.
