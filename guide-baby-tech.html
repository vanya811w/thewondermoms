The Baby Tech Gadgets That Were Actually Useful (And The Ones That Were Expensive Junk)

My husband is an engineer who bought literally every baby gadget on the market. A year and a half later, here's our honest scorecard.

Full disclosure: this post contains affiliate links; if you buy through them we may earn a small commission at no extra cost. We only recommend what we genuinely used.

When I was pregnant, my husband Mike announced he'd handled "the data side of parenting." Within a week he had a spreadsheet called INFANT_ACQUISITION_v2.xlsx, with columns for price, projected utility, and a weighted score. I laughed at him. Then the boxes started arriving — by the time our daughter was born, the nursery looked like the showroom floor of a consumer electronics expo. So yes, we really did test everything. Here's what earned its keep and what's collecting dust.

The Nanit Pro — the one I'd buy again tonight

The Nanit Pro was the first thing Mike set up, and it's the one purchase I'd defend to anyone. The breathing band looked like a tiny surveillance operation taped to her onesie, and yes, we both checked the app from the couch like idiots. But the sleep tracking told us things our sleep-deprived brains never would have figured out — like how she only slept well at exactly 68 degrees, or that her 9 p.m. "false starts" happened at a suspiciously precise 42 minutes. The night video is the best we tried. Honest gripes: the smart insights sit behind a subscription now, and it's Wi-Fi only, so it dies with the internet. Still, we used it every single night for 15 months.

The Owlet Dream Sock — the one we fought about

At 3:19 a.m., eight weeks in, the Owlet base station lit up red and started wailing — we were in her room before the alarm finished its first note. She was fine. False alarm. I wanted to box it up that night. But there's another side: three weeks later, at a 2 a.m. feed, I saw the sock's oxygen number dip and, against my judgment, called the pediatrician. It turned out to be reflux-related apnea — nothing catastrophic, but something we needed to know. But I slept through her first year with this thing on her foot, and I can't price that. I still recommend it — with eyes wide open.

The Hatch Rest+ — the boring hero

The most boring gadget we own has been the most used, by a mile. The Hatch Rest+ has played white noise for every nap and bedtime since day one, and it survived a power outage thanks to the backup battery. Now that she's a toddler, it's a sleep trainer — the little sun glows green at 7 a.m. and she actually waits for it. The app means I can dim the light from bed instead of getting up — worth the $89 alone. This is the gadget I tell every new parent to buy before the baby is even born.

The Baby Brezza — the formula miracle

I rolled my eyes when Mike unboxed a $199 machine whose entire job is mixing powder and water. Then I used it at 2 a.m. Press a button, warm bottle, done — no clumps, no foam, no measuring when you can barely see. The catch: you have to clean the funnel every four bottles or the powder gets weird, and it's another appliance squatting on your counter. But for formula-feeding parents, this is the single best $199 we spent — we only retired it when she switched to whole milk at a year.

The Momcozy bottle washer — the expensive junk

And now, the flop. The Momcozy KleanPal Pro bottle washer cost $119, and I blame late-night Instagram ads for its existence in our home. In theory: load bottles, it washes, sterilizes, and dries them. In reality: by the time we loaded it and ran a cycle, I could have washed the bottles by hand and started the next load of laundry. It served us maybe four times total. It currently holds our recycling. I couldn't wait to say I told you so. Mike, to his credit, called it "a good experiment." The one column he never put in that spreadsheet: will we actually use this?

If you're building a registry right now: buy the monitor, get a sound machine early, and know that gadgets multiplying on your counter do not equal better sleep. Data is fun. Sleep is better.

🛒 Where to buy: the Nanit Pro monitor ($299), the Owlet Dream Sock ($299), the Hatch Rest+ sound machine ($89), the Baby Brezza Formula Pro ($199) — and if you want a $119 paperweight, the Momcozy bottle washer.
