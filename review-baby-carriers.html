I Wore My Baby 4 Hours A Day For 6 Months. One Carrier Saved My Back, Two Collected Dust.

How I went from "babywearing is overrated" to owning five carriers and having very strong opinions about waistbands.

Before I had my daughter, I thought babywearing was for granola moms who went to drum circles. By week 3 of maternity leave, I would have worn her to the bathroom if I could have figured out the strap configuration. When you have a baby who refuses to be put down and a dishwasher that needs unloading, a carrier isn't a lifestyle accessory — it's survival equipment.

Over six months of near-daily use (my daughter was, let's say, "velcro-adjacent"), I tested five carriers. Here's the whole messy truth.

The Five I Tried

  • Ergobaby Omni 360 — $179, all-position, newborn to 45 lbs — 4.8★ (24,000+ reviews)
  • BabyBjörn One Air — $189, mesh fabric, 4 carrying positions — 4.7★ (7,000+ reviews)
  • Tula Explore — $159, ergonomic seat, 6 positions — 4.8★ (5,000+ reviews)
  • Infantino Flip 4-in-1 — $29, budget convertible — 4.6★ (35,000+ reviews)
  • Boba Wrap Classic — $39, stretchy wrap style — 4.7★ (20,000+ reviews)

Ergobaby Omni 360: The Daily Driver

This is the Toyota Camry of baby carriers. It's not the most exciting, but it works every single time and you never worry about it breaking. The lumbar support pad is the hero feature — it redistributes weight across your hips instead of dumping everything on your shoulders. After a 3-hour museum outing with a 15-pound baby, my back felt tired but not destroyed. That's a win.

The downside: it's bulky. Like, "takes up half the diaper bag" bulky. And on hot days, even the "cool air mesh" version gets sweaty. Both you and baby will emerge damp. But for everyday reliability, nothing beat it.

BabyBjörn One Air: The Sleek One

The BabyBjörn was the carrier I wanted to love. It's beautiful — the mesh fabric looks modern, it comes in tasteful colors, and it's genuinely lighter than the Ergo. The buckles click satisfyingly. You feel like a Scandinavian design parent when you wear it.

But. The waistband is narrower than the Ergo, which matters after about 45 minutes. My lower back started complaining by the end of a grocery run. And the seat isn't quite as deep, so my daughter's legs didn't get the full "M" shape that babywearing educators recommend for hip development. It's fine for short errands. For all-day wear? My back voted no.

Tula Explore: The One I Didn't Expect To Love

I bought this used on Facebook Marketplace for $75 expecting to hate it and resell it. Instead, it became my second-favorite. The seat is slightly softer than the Ergo, the patterns are actually cute (mine had little foxes), and it packs down smaller. The hood is built in for nursing privacy or sun protection. At 8 months, my daughter preferred the Tula over the Ergo — I think the softer fabric felt cozier to her. If you find one on sale, buy it.

Infantino Flip: The $29 Question

Thirty five thousand reviews can't be wrong, right? For $29, this carrier works. It holds the baby. The buckles mostly stay buckled. But the straps are scratchy, the padding is minimal, and after about 20 minutes, you understand why it costs $29. I kept this in my trunk as the emergency backup carrier — and used it exactly twice. Both times I wished I'd remembered to bring the Ergo. If $29 is your budget, it will function. But if you can stretch to even the Tula or a used Ergo, do it.

Boba Wrap: The Newborn Cocoon

For the first 10 weeks, this was the only thing that worked. The Boba is one long piece of stretchy fabric you wrap around yourself like a fabric burrito. It takes about 5 attempts to learn the technique, and then it becomes muscle memory. My daughter would fall asleep in it within 90 seconds. The stretchy fabric keeps them snug against your chest in a way structured carriers don't replicate.

The problem: once your baby hits about 15 pounds, the fabric sags. You have to retighten every 30 minutes. By 3 months, my daughter would stretch her legs and the whole wrap would shift. But for newborns? Absolute magic. I'm keeping mine for the next baby.

What I'd Actually Buy Again

Ergobaby Omni 360 for everyday — it's the workhorse. Find it used if $179 stings. They hold value well and you can resell for about $80 when you're done.

Boba Wrap for the newborn phase, no question. $39 to have both hands free during the fourth trimester is a bargain.

Tula Explore if you find it on sale and want something softer/cozier than the Ergo. It's a lovely carrier.

Skip the BabyBjörn unless you only plan to babywear for <30 minutes at a time. Your back will thank you.

Skip the Infantino unless $29 is your absolute max. Save up an extra $50 and buy a used Ergo instead.

🛒 Where to buy: All available on Amazon. Also check Facebook Marketplace and Mercari for used Ergos and Tulas — baby carriers depreciate fast and most are in great condition.
