Having A Winter Baby: The Cold-Weather Gear We Couldn't Have Lived Without

Our daughter arrived January 14 in Chicago, during a stretch where the high was 3 degrees. Here's what actually got us through it.

Full disclosure: this post contains affiliate links; if you buy through them we may earn a small commission at no extra cost. We only recommend what we genuinely used.

Our daughter was born on January 14, and I'll never forget the walk from the hospital to the car: wind chill negative seventeen, my husband gripping the car seat like it contained a Fabergé egg, me limping across a parking lot that hadn't been plowed. Leaving the house was a 45-minute production — base layer, sleeper, bunting, cover, and me sweating in a coat I could barely zip because the baby needed 47 layers and I needed three. But we survived, and we learned exactly which gear does the job. Here it is, in order of importance.

The car seat cover — non-negotiable

First, the safety note that matters more than any product: thick puffy stuff does not go inside the car seat under the straps — the harness has to be snug against your baby's body, or the padding compresses in a crash. That's why the JJ Cole cover goes over the carrier: it zips around the outside like a tiny toasty cave while the harness stays tight underneath. The sprint from front door to car went from terrifying to routine. It also lived on the stroller for every Target run. Buy it before the baby does.

Zutano booties — socks are a myth

You learn quickly that baby socks are a government conspiracy. They fall off in minutes and end up in the diaper pail or the void between the car seat and the seat belt. Zutano fleece booties have snaps — actual snaps around the ankle — so they stay on. They're fleece, they fit over double socks for polar vortex days, and the grippers kept working once she started pulling up. We went through three pairs because we loved them, and I found the "missing" sock in the diaper pail six months later.

The Hanna Andersson fleece bunting — the coat of the century

For actual walks — the ones that saved my mental health that winter — the Hanna Andersson fleece bunting was the star. It's a full-body fleece suit with a zipper from neck to toe, covered hands and feet, essentially a wearable sleeping bag with sleeves. We bought it directly from Hanna Andersson because that's where they sell it, and at around $44 it was our biggest single splurge and our best one. Pro tip: size up — she wore it from January through April. Every walk ended with us unzipping a rosy-cheeked, slightly steaming baby at the coffee shop, like a tiny happy dumpling.

Zipper footed sleepers — the 3 a.m. essential

You will change a diaper at 3 a.m., half asleep, in the dark. I am begging you: buy the two-way zipper sleepers. The zipper opens from the bottom, so you change the diaper without unswaddling or facing fifteen snaps at 3 a.m. The Simple Joys by Carter's fleece two-packs run about $18, and we lived in them — four sets in rotation, washed so often they got softer every week. Zipper from the bottom, always. Snaps are the enemy.

The stroller bunting bag — the February walk enabler

The stroller footmuff is the piece of gear that made long walks possible. The 7AM Enfant Stroller Blanket 212 zips around the entire stroller seat — baby goes in, fleece-lined, zips to the chest, windproof outside. In February, with the wind off the lake, she took her best naps of the season in that bag. It has a removable back panel so the car seat harness works with it too. It was the one we used almost daily for four months straight.

The winter-baby checklist

So, if you're due between November and March: a cover for the car seat, zipper sleepers for the house, a bunting for walks, booties that stay on, and the footmuff for the stroller. One rule we lived by: if her cheeks were pink and her hands were warm, she was fine — cold hands in a carrier are a circulation thing, not a freezing thing. You'll get weirdly proud of how fast you can get a baby into a car seat in single digits. The right gear makes it not terrible.
