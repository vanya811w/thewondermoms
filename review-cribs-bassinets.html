We Started With A $35 IKEA Crib And Upgraded To The Snoo. Here's What Actually Mattered.

My husband said I was crazy for renting a $159/month bassinet. Then we all started sleeping through the night and he became the Snoo's biggest fan.

The Sleep Deprivation Tax Is Real

Before I had a baby, I remember judging parents who bought the Snoo. I'd see the price tag -- $1,695 for a bassinet, or $159 a month to rent it -- and think, "That is the most absurd waste of money I've ever seen. It's a bassinet. With a motor. My grandmother put my dad to sleep in a dresser drawer and he turned out fine."

Then I had a baby who didn't sleep. And I learned something very important about the baby gear industry: sometimes the expensive thing actually is worth it, and sometimes the cheap thing is just as good. The trick is figuring out which is which before you've spent a thousand dollars learning the hard way.

We went through four sleep setups in the first six months of my daughter's life. Here's what I learned from each one.

IKEA Sniglar Crib ($99 with mattress) — The Reality Check

I want to start with the most wholesome truth in all of baby gear: an IKEA crib is a perfectly good crib. The Sniglar is $99 including the mattress (the $69 mattress that IKEA sells meets all safety standards and is firmer than most expensive crib mattresses, which is actually what you want to reduce SIDS risk). It's a plain white rectangle. It holds the baby. That's it. That's all a crib needs to do.

We set up the Sniglar in the nursery when I was 36 weeks pregnant. I felt so responsible, so prepared. I had a Pinterest board of nursery decor ideas. I hung a mobile. I folded tiny blankets on the shelf. It was adorable.

And then Ellie was born, and it turned out she had zero interest in sleeping in her beautiful Pinterest-ready crib. She wanted to be swaddled, held, and preferably bounced rhythmically while listening to white noise at exactly the right pitch. The $99 crib sat there looking cute, completely unused, for two months while she slept in a bassinet in our room.

At about 5 months, we moved her into the Sniglar and it worked great. It's a crib. She slept in it. Mission accomplished. If you buy nothing but an IKEA crib from Facebook Marketplace for $35 like my friend did, your baby will be fine. The crib is not the variable that determines whether your baby sleeps.

Halo BassiNest Swivel ($249, 4.6 stars, 8K reviews) — The Roommate Crib

After the "baby won't sleep in the nursery" realization hit, we needed a bassinet for our room. I'd seen the Halo BassiNest recommended in every "what to buy for a newborn" list and I understood why the second I set it up.

The whole thing swivels. The base sits next to your bed, and the bassinet part swings right over your mattress. So when Ellie woke up at 2 AM making hungry sounds, I could just reach over, swivel the bassinet toward me, scoop her out, nurse her without getting out of bed, and put her back. The side of the bassinet drops down (it's mesh, so it's breathable but the side lowers for easy access), which meant I didn't have to lift her over a high edge.

That drop-down side is the feature you don't know you need until you've spent a week leaning over a traditional bassinet with a c-section incision, cursing your past self for not buying the Halo. My friend who had a c-section said the Halo was the single most important purchase she made.

The Halo is not fancy. It doesn't rock itself. It doesn't detect crying. It's a bassinet that swivels and has a drop-down side. But that's honestly all most parents need. At $249, it's the best non-robotic bassinet on the market, and I'd recommend it to anyone who isn't ready to spend Snoo money but wants a solid co-sleeping setup.

Graco Sense2Snooze ($139, 4.4 stars, 3K reviews) — The Budget Tech Option

The Graco Sense2Snooze is trying to be what the Snoo is, but at a fraction of the price. It has a built-in sensor that detects when the baby is fussing and responds with vibration and sounds. The whole thing soothes automatically, which sounds amazing in theory.

In practice, it worked okay. The sensor is pretty good at detecting when the baby is starting to stir, and the vibration and white noise response would sometimes settle Ellie back down before she fully woke up. I'd say it prevented about 30% of night wakings that would have required my intervention.

But the vibration isn't the same as the Snoo's motion. The Graco vibrates, which is a different sensation. Some babies love it; Ellie was lukewarm. And the white noise options are limited compared to a dedicated white noise machine. Still, for $139, it's a reasonable middle ground if you want some automated soothing without spending $1,695.

Snoo Smart Sleeper ($1,695 purchase / $159/month rental, 4.7 stars, 5K reviews) — The Robot Nanny

Okay. The Snoo. The bassinet that broke the internet and my bank account.

Here's what the Snoo actually does: it's a bassinet with a built-in swaddle that clips into the mattress. When the baby starts to fuss, sensors detect the crying and the Snoo starts rocking and playing white noise. If the baby calms down, it rocks gently. If the baby keeps crying, it rocks faster and louder until the baby settles or you pick them up. You can control it all from your phone. There's an app. There are five levels of motion. It's wild.

We rented it. $159 a month, which felt like a lot until I calculated what I'd pay for a sleep consultant, which was more. We got it when Ellie was 6 weeks old and I was hallucinating from sleep deprivation.

The first night, she slept a four-hour stretch. Then a three-hour stretch. That's seven hours of broken sleep, which felt like a full night's rest compared to what I'd been getting (two hours at a time, max). By the end of the first week, she was averaging 5-6 hour stretches. I cried. Not sad crying. "I forgot what it felt like to sleep for more than two hours" crying.

The Snoo worked for us. It gave us approximately two extra hours of sleep per night, cumulative. My husband, who had called the Snoo "an overpriced laundry basket with Bluetooth" before we got it, became its biggest advocate. He'd literally stand over the Snoo watching it rock the baby back to sleep, whispering "good robot" like it was a sentient being.

But here's the honest truth: the Snoo is a luxury, not a necessity. Babies have slept in cardboard boxes and they turned out fine. The Snoo makes life easier, especially in those brutal newborn weeks, but it won't fix all sleep problems. Some babies just don't like the Snoo. And at $159 a month (or $1,695 to buy), you have to decide if that's worth it for your family.

For us, renting for months 2-4 was perfect. By month 5, Ellie was too big for the Snoo anyway (they max out at about 6 months or when the baby can roll over). We transitioned her to the IKEA crib with a standard sleep sack, and she did fine. The Snoo bought us time during the hardest months.

What Actually Mattered: The Bottom Line

If I had to do it all over again, here's what I'd buy:

  • For months 0-4: Rent a Snoo if you can afford $159/month. It genuinely makes a difference in those early weeks when sleep is currency. If you can't, get the Halo BassiNest Swivel. The swivel feature alone saves your back and your sanity.
  • For months 4+: Any safe crib that fits your budget. The IKEA Sniglar is $99 and works perfectly. A Facebook Marketplace crib for $35 also works perfectly. A $1,200 Pottery Barn crib also holds a mattress. Do not stress about the crib.
  • The 80% solution: Halo BassiNest + a good swaddle (we loved the Ollie) + a white noise machine. That combo gets you about 80% of the Snoo's benefit for way less money.
  • The full solution: Snoo rental + IKEA crib. Spend on the thing that actively helps your baby sleep (the Snoo), save on the thing that just holds the mattress (the crib).

At the end of the day, your baby will sleep eventually. It might take weeks, it might take months, but they will figure it out. The question is just how much help you want along the way. And there's no shame in hiring the robot nanny if you can swing it.

Where to buy: The Halo BassiNest Swivel is $249 on Amazon. The Graco Sense2Snooze is $139. The Snoo Smart Sleeper is $1,695 to buy or $159/month to rent directly from Happiest Baby. The IKEA Sniglar is available at IKEA stores for $99.
