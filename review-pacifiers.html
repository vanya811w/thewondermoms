My Baby Had STRONG Opinions About Pacifiers. We Tried 6 Brands Before She Approved One.

I thought a pacifier was just a pacifier. Then my daughter spent three weeks methodically rejecting every single one I bought until she found her one true love.

Before I Had Kids, I Thought Pacifiers Were All The Same

I distinctly remember standing in the Target baby aisle at 32 weeks pregnant, staring at a wall of pacifiers, and thinking, "How hard can this be? It's a little rubber nub. The baby sucks it. Everyone's happy."

Oh, sweet summer-child version of myself. You had no idea what was coming.

My daughter, Ellie, was born with apparently very sophisticated opinions about what went in her mouth. From the moment the nurse popped a hospital pacifier in there during her first heel prick, I thought we were set. She sucked contentedly for about 45 seconds, then pursed her little lips like she'd been served a bad oyster and spat it out with surprising velocity for a three-hour-old human.

Thus began our three-month-long pacifier odyssey. We tried six brands. We spent roughly $80 on little pieces of silicone and latex. We watched YouTube reviews at 2 AM. And eventually, we found the one. Here's what we learned about every pacifier we tried, so hopefully you don't have to turn your nursery into a pacifier testing lab like we did.

Philips Avent Soothie — The Hospital Standard ($5 for 2, 4.8 stars, 42K reviews)

This is the one they hand you in the hospital. It's the classic "orthodontic" shape that looks like a yellow cartoon ghost with a hole at the bottom. It's one single piece of silicone, which sounds boring but is honestly its superpower.

The one-piece construction means there's nowhere for slobber or milk to get trapped. You can throw these things in the dishwasher without any parts separating or getting lost. We ran ours through the sterilizer so many times they started to look a little tired, but they never once fell apart.

Ellie's verdict: She took it about 40% of the time. When she was in a good mood and not overly tired, she'd happily suck on the Soothie for a good twenty minutes. When she was overstimulated or cranky, she acted like I was trying to feed her a lemon and screamed until I took it away. So it was our reliable backup, but not her soulmate.

For $5 for a two-pack though? You should absolutely have these in your diaper bag. They're what the hospital uses because they work for most babies and they're impossible to break.

MAM Night — The Glow-In-The-Dark Genius ($6 for 2, 4.7 stars, 30K reviews)

Okay, I need to talk about the glow-in-the-dark feature on these because it is the single most practical baby product innovation I have ever encountered.

You know that thing where you're stumbling into the nursery at 3 AM, half-asleep, and the pacifier has fallen out of the baby's mouth and is now somewhere in the crib abyss? Usually you end up patting around in the dark like a blind person searching for their glasses, and either you find it slobber-side-up on your hand, or you give up and grab a backup from the counter.

With MAM Night, the pacifier GLOWS. Not in a gimmicky way that fades after fifteen minutes. It genuinely stays visible all night. You just set it under the light for a bit before bed and it charges up. At 3 AM, you look in the crib and there's a soft green glow pinpointing exactly where the pacifier landed. It saved me approximately 47 panic attacks and countless fumbling-under-the-crib moments.

The MAM has a symmetrical nipple that's flatter on one side, which is supposed to mimic the shape of a mother's nipple during breastfeeding. The shield has these big air holes so baby's skin can breathe. Ellie actually liked this one more than the Soothie — she'd keep it in for a solid hour sometimes. But the nipple shape is a little more rigid, and I noticed she'd sometimes fuss trying to keep it positioned.

Still, this was our runner-up. If it weren't for the winner below, MAM would have been the champion of our household.

NUK Comfy — The Dentist's Choice ($5 for 2, 4.6 stars, 18K reviews)

NUK makes the orthodontic pacifier that most pediatric dentists recommend. The shape is supposed to support natural oral development and reduce the risk of dental issues later on. The nipple is flat on the bottom and rounded on top, which supposedly fits the palate better.

Here's the thing: I really wanted Ellie to like the NUK because I'm a sucker for anything that promises better dental outcomes. I spent way too much money on orthodontic retainers as a teenager, and the idea of preventing even one future dental appointment was appealing.

Ellie's response: She tolerated it. The shield has this cute ergonomic shape that fits her face nicely, and it's lightweight. But she never seemed truly happy with it. She'd keep it in her mouth for a few minutes, then let it fall out and look at me like, "Is that all you've got?"

The NUK is a solid, well-made pacifier. If your baby isn't as picky as mine, this is probably a great option, especially for the price. But for our little diva, it was a polite no.

Nanobebe Flexy — The One She Threw Across The Room ($7, 4.5 stars, 8K reviews)

I had high hopes for this one. The Nanobebe Flexy has this beautiful, modern design with a flexible, curved shield that's supposed to contour to the baby's face. The nipple is super soft and squishy. The whole thing looks like something Apple would make if they got into the pacifier business.

I handed it to Ellie with great optimism. She took it. She sucked twice. And then she made a face I can only describe as "you have personally offended me" and started screaming like I'd tricked her into eating a lemon wedge.

I tried three more times on different days. Every single time, she rejected it within seconds. My friend's baby, on the other hand, is absolutely obsessed with the Nanobebe and won't touch anything else. So clearly this is a polarizing pacifier.

The curved shield is innovative, and I can see how it would work great for some babies. But for us, it was a hard pass. I ended up giving our pack to my friend, and her baby is now best friends with them. Your mileage may vary dramatically here.

Tommee Tippee Ultra-Light — The Lightweight Champion ($6 for 2, 4.6 stars, 14K reviews)

These are called Ultra-Light for a reason. They weigh practically nothing. The whole pacifier is vented and airy, with this super thin silicone nipple that's closer to the texture of a real nipple than any other pacifier we tried.

The idea is that because it's so light, it stays in the baby's mouth better. And honestly? That checks out. When Ellie actually accepted this one, it stayed put longer than most. The lightweight design means gravity isn't working against you.

The shield is also really flexible and curves away from the face, which I appreciated because Ellie has those chunky baby cheeks that everything seems to dig into. The Tommee Tippee left no red marks at all.

Ellie's verdict: She liked it a solid 60% of the time. Better than the Soothie, not quite as good as the MAM. It became one of our rotation pacifiers, and I'd recommend it to any parent whose baby struggles with heavier pacifiers falling out of their mouth.

The Winner: Philips Avent Soothie (With A Twist)

After three months of testing, you know which pacifier Ellie ultimately decided was her one and only? The Philips Avent Soothie. The basic one. The hospital one. The one I bought first.

But here's the thing — she wouldn't take the 0-3 month size. Oh no. She only accepted the 6-18 month size, even when she was two months old. She wanted the bigger nipple. She knew what she wanted, and it was more pacifier.

So our final setup was: Philips Avent Soothie in the larger size as the daily driver, MAM Night for overnight use (the glow feature is truly unbeatable), and the Tommee Tippee Ultra-Light as a backup in the diaper bag.

Where to buy: All of these pacifiers are available on Amazon. The Philips Avent Soothie is $5 for a 2-pack. The MAM Night is $6 for 2. The NUK Comfy is $5 for 2. The Nanobebe Flexy is $7. The Tommee Tippee Ultra-Light is $6 for 2.
