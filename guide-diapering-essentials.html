Diapering Setup: The Products That Make Changing 3,000 Diapers Slightly Less Terrible

Eight changes a day, 365 days. I did the math, and here's the gear that survived the year.

Full disclosure: this post contains affiliate links. If you buy through them, we may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. We only recommend what we genuinely used.

Somewhere around 3 a.m., mid-blowout, I remember thinking: I should count the diapers. So I did. Eight changes a day times 365 days — 2,920 diapers in the first year. Nearly 3,000 opportunities to mess up, miss, or be peed on. You cannot make changing diapers fun, but you can absolutely make it less terrible. These are the five things that carried us through, and the ones I'd happily set on fire before recommending.

The Ubbi pail — the smell containment unit

Let's start with the thing nobody warns you about: the smell. We had a gifted diaper genie with its fancy refill bags, and our living room still smelled like a zoo exhibit by Wednesday. We switched to the steel Ubbi pail ($69) because "odor-locking" sounded like marketing. It is not marketing. That thing ate a week of newborn poops and my mother-in-law genuinely asked if we composted. The detail that matters at 2 a.m.: it takes any regular trash bag, no proprietary refills. It's heavier than the plastic pails, and the lid takes a firm shove, but smell-wise it is the difference between a nursery and a crime scene.

The Keekaroo Peanut — the $129 piece of plastic I'd pay double for

We borrowed a fabric changing pad with a cover from a friend. By the second month I was doing pad-cover laundry every other day — pee, poo, the mystery stains, all of it. I bought the Keekaroo Peanut Changer out of spite and never looked back. It's a solid foam peanut with a wipe-clean surface. Every blowout, every pee fountain, every mystery substance — one baby wipe and it's gone. No laundry, no covers, no surprise. It's cold on the belly at first, so we lay a muslin over it in winter, but that's the whole downside. Ours is also currently being used as a toy perch, a teething station, and a stage for the world's smallest rock concert. It looks brand new after a year.

WaterWipes — the only wipes her skin tolerated

Our daughter's thighs turned pink with literally every wipe brand we tried — the fancy ones, the cheap ones, the "sensitive" ones. Our pediatrician finally said: strip it all down to water. WaterWipes are 99.9% water with a drop of fruit extract, and that was the end of the rash saga. They're more expensive per wipe, which hurts when you're going through a pack every three days, so we buy the giant 12-pack boxes and restock on subscribe-and-save. If your baby's skin is sensitive, skip the trial-and-error phase and just start here.

Aquaphor — the $11 MVP

Our pediatrician's exact words: "a thick layer at every single change, no shame in slathering." The Aquaphor Baby tube ($11) is the workhorse — it prevents rash, fixes the drool rash on her chin, handles chapped knuckles, and in a pinch moisturizes my own elbows. One tube lasted us four months, which makes it the cheapest thing on this entire list per use. You do not need a fancy zinc paste for daily wear. You need Aquaphor and commitment.

The diaper caddy — the unsung hero

Nothing kills a good day faster than discovering the wipes are twenty feet away while your child is mid-roll and mid-tantrum. We have three of these rope diaper caddies ($18 each): one on the changing station, one in the living room, one that migrates to the car and the backyard. They hold diapers, wipes, cream, a change of clothes, and — in our house — a spare pacifier and the TV remote that always disappears. The rope ones are light, washable, and durable enough that our toddler uses one as a basket to haul toys around. The best $18 in the whole nursery.

What to skip

For the record: the wipe warmer, the scented liners, the diaper stacker that holds six diapers (you will change them all in a two-hour span, trust me), and the $40 "designer" diaper bag. You need containment, wipes, cream, and somewhere for the smell to go. That's the entire job — plus a snack for you behind the caddy. You've earned it.

🛒 Where to buy: the Ubbi steel diaper pail ($69), the Keekaroo Peanut Changer ($129), WaterWipes 12-pack ($25), Aquaphor Baby Healing Ointment ($11), and a rope diaper caddy ($18).
