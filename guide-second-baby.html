What I'm Buying Again For Baby #2 (And What I'm Definitely Not)

A second-time mom's honest audit of what survived baby #1 — and what got tossed in the donate pile.

Baby number two is due in eight weeks, which means I have officially reached the most dangerous phase of second-time pregnancy: the stash audit. Our garage is a museum of everything we bought for baby #1, and I've spent three weekends crawling through it, making two piles. Keep. Donate. The donate pile is bigger than I expected. The keep pile is smaller — and more interesting, because it's full of brand new boxes.

Here's the thing nobody warns you about: some baby gear ages out before your baby does. Elastic goes limp. Silicone gets cloudy. Pacifiers... exist. So yes, I'm buying again — but this time with the brutal honesty that only eighteen months of actual use can give you. Here's what earned a repeat purchase, and why.

Halo SleepSack — New One, Same Love

Our first Halo SleepSack ($27) survived sixteen months of washing and still looks decent, but the swaddle wings are stretched out and honestly? It's done its time. Sleep sacks are the single best thing we bought for baby #1 — no blankets, no loose bedding, no 3 AM panic about SIDS guidelines. The second one is already washed and waiting in the crib. If you're a new parent reading this, buy one before you need one, not at 11 PM when your baby refuses the swaddle for the first time.

New Bottle Nipples (Yes, They Expire)

I did not know bottle nipples had a lifespan until our pediatrician mentioned that old silicone can get sticky and start breaking down — and then I squeezed the ones from baby #1 and yep, clouded and tacky. A pack of replacement nipples ($6) is cheap insurance, and they're one of the few baby items where the new version is genuinely better than the hand-me-down. The old ones went in the trash, not the donate pile. You don't donate used nipples. You just don't.

Fresh Spectra Pump Parts

I pumped for fourteen months with my Spectra, and I have complicated feelings about every minute of it, but the pump itself is a tank. The parts, though? The duckbill valves and membranes stretch out and lose suction after a few months, and I definitely wasn't replacing them as often as I should have. Fresh Spectra parts ($25) are going in the hospital bag this time, so my output doesn't quietly drop because of worn-out silicone while I'm running on three hours of sleep. Learn from my mistake: replace the valves, don't just wash them harder.

The Skip Hop Doubleplay — The MVP I Underrated

I bought the Skip Hop Doubleplay play mat ($75) on sale, mostly because it matched the nursery. It ended up being the most-used baby item we owned, bar none. Tummy time, play gym, the mat we dragged into the kitchen while I cooked dinner, the mat that survived a teething toddler gnawing on its edges — it did everything. Baby #1 used it daily from week two to month sixteen. I already unboxed the new one for baby #2, and honestly, it's the only "rebuy" that felt like a no-brainer.

Fresh Pacifiers (Because Ew)

Here's my least glamorous confession: baby #1's pacifiers have been dropped in parking lots, chewed by the dog (twice), and bleached into oblivion. A new pack of pacifiers ($5) costs less than a drive-through coffee. Replacing them for baby #2 was the easiest purchase decision of my life. Buy new. Don't overthink it. Your future self at 3 AM will thank you.

What's NOT Coming Back

For balance, here's the other pile, because I want you to know this list is honest: the wipe warmer (nobody has time for warm wipes at 3 AM, and it's a mold risk anyway), the bottle sterilizer (our dishwasher's sanitize cycle does the same job), and the "nursery glider" that my husband and I both hate because it squeaks with every rock. And the four — yes, four — different swaddles I bought before figuring out she only liked one. That's about $120 of lessons learned, and they're all going to the garage sale.

Second-time mom math is simple: buy what actually survived the first baby, replace what's worn out, and let the garage sale take the rest. Eight weeks to go. We're ready.

🛒 Where to buy: All the rebuys are on Amazon: the Halo SleepSack ($27), replacement bottle nipples ($6), Spectra pump parts ($25), the Skip Hop Doubleplay mat ($75), and fresh pacifiers ($5).
