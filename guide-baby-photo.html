How I Got Professional-Looking Baby Photos At Home (With Just My Phone And These Props)

The newborn photographer quoted me $450. I spent $99 instead — and one of these photos ended up with 40,000 likes.

When I got the quote for newborn photos — $450 for 45 minutes, plus $75 if I wanted to be in any of them — I cried. Partly hormones, mostly math. My husband had just been laid off, and $450 was a week of groceries in our house.

So I did what any stubborn first-time mom does: I decided to fake it. My phone, our living room, and a half-baked plan I came up with at 2 a.m. during a feed. The first attempt looked like a crime scene photo. Baby purple with rage, my shadow covering half the frame, overhead lighting making everyone look slightly jaundiced. I posted it in a Facebook group asking for "tips" and got four comments of "bless your heart."

But I'm not someone who quits over a bad first draft. I watched three YouTube videos, bought five cheap props, and by month two people genuinely thought I'd hired a professional. By month six, one of my monthly photos took off — 40k likes, two feature posts in big parenting accounts, and my cousin asking if I "knew someone" who could shoot her baby.

The magic was never a fancy camera. It was a consistent setup. Same spot in the living room, same time of day (9 a.m., right after the first nap), same five props. Here's exactly what I used.

The Ring Light That Made It Look Expensive

The single biggest upgrade, and at $29 it felt like a splurge. This ring light is the reason every photo has that soft, even glow instead of harsh shadows. I set it on the warmest setting, propped my phone on a stack of books (yes, really), and suddenly we looked like a lifestyle ad. I used it every day for months, and it also made video calls to grandma about 100% less terrifying.

The Milestone Blanket

This was my "do the exact same thing every month" anchor. I laid out this milestone blanket in the same corner, and because the design is busy but symmetrical, the photos read as "intentional" instead of "mom with a phone." A $22 investment I used twelve times, every single month, rain or shine. Pro tip: wash it once before the first shoot, then lint roll it between uses — wrinkles show up in photos way more than they do in real life.

Wooden Milestone Cards

These wooden milestone cards saved me because they double as a memory aid. I have friends who added text with apps, and it looks... like an app. The wood photographs beautifully in natural light, and the white-on-wood lettering is readable even in a tiny thumbnail. Also, baby can't push them over the way she could a paper one. Ask me how I know.

Fabric Backdrop

For $18, this fabric backdrop bought me a blank studio wall. I hung it with two command hooks and a curtain rod. It reads as "seamless background," not "cheap fabric," because it doesn't wrinkle the way bedsheets do. This was the prop that made the difference between "cute photo" and "people asking what camera I use."

Letter Board

My favorite for the outtakes — the shots that actually went viral. A letter board I'd swap out every month ("4 months of chaos," "sleep is for the weak"). The candid shots of her laughing at me pushing letters around got more engagement than any posed photo. Go figure.

The Swaddle That Did 90% of the Work

If you want that "newborn in a cocoon" look, this is the prop. This stretchy swaddle wrap keeps baby bundled so you get those clean, contained poses without wrestling a flailing newborn for 40 minutes. I learned that the hard way during my first shoot, which I spent mostly trying to keep a wiggly infant in a basket. Never again.

My Monthly Photo Routine (Copy It If You Want)

9 a.m., baby fed exactly 30 minutes prior, ring light on warm, backdrop hung, blanket smooth, cards ready. Ten minutes of shooting, then snacks for both of us. I took 20 photos a month and was lucky to get 3 good ones — that's just the reality of photographing a baby. But the ones that worked? They're on my wall, they're in the annual family calendar, and they're my favorite thing on my phone.

If you're on the fence about hiring a photographer: do whatever feels right. But if you're like me and the quote made your chest tight — do it yourself. You'll end up with more photos, a monthly ritual you'll genuinely miss when it ends, and one day your kid will ask why there are 12 nearly-identical photos of her on a blanket. And you'll say, "Because your dad lost his job, sweetheart."
