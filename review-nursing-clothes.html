I Bought Nursing Clothes From Amazon, Target, And A Fancy Brand. The $28 Amazon Dress Won.

I spent $200 on "designer" nursing clothes and my favorite piece was the three-pack of Amazon dresses that cost less than one fancy top.

I Was Not Prepared For The Clothes Situation

Everyone tells you about the sleep deprivation. Everyone warns you about the birth. Nobody prepares you for the fact that for the first six months of your baby's life, you will need to access your breasts approximately every two hours, often in public, sometimes in a parked car, and your regular wardrobe was not designed for this.

I remember standing in my closet when my daughter was about two weeks old, crying because none of my shirts could accommodate a nursing session without essentially undressing from the waist up. The pullover sweaters I loved? Traps. The button-down blouses? So annoying to unbutton one-handed while holding a screaming infant. Everything I owned was suddenly useless.

So I did what any sleep-deprived new mom would do: I went on an Amazon shopping spree at 1 AM. I ordered from multiple brands. I spent way too much money in the name of "self-care" and "convenience." And then I compared everything side by side to figure out what was actually worth it. Here's what I learned.

Bearsland Nursing Dresses 3-Pack ($42 for 3, 4.3 stars, 15K reviews) — The Unlikely Champion

Okay, I need to start with the product I had the lowest expectations for, because it ended up being my absolute favorite. I bought the Bearsland 3-pack of nursing dresses on a whim because the price was absurdly low — $42 for three dresses works out to $14 per dress. I was fully prepared for them to be cheap, flimsy, and weirdly shaped.

They are none of those things. They're actually great.

The dresses are made of a soft, stretchy jersey material that's thick enough not to show every lump and bump (important in the postpartum months when your body is still doing mysterious things). They have a scoop neck with built-in nursing access — you just pull the neckline down or to the side. No clips, no zippers, no complicated mechanisms. Just stretchy fabric that moves out of the way when you need it to and snaps back into place when you're done.

The fit is flattering in a "casual but put-together" way. I wore these to pediatrician appointments, to Target runs, to my mom's group meetups, and honestly? Nobody could tell they were nursing dresses. They just looked like regular stretchy knit dresses. I got compliments from friends who had no idea I was wearing what was essentially a $14 Amazon dress.

After six months of regular wear (I rotated through all three constantly), they've held up well. A little fading in the black one, but no pilling, no loose threads, no stretching out. For $14 a dress, that's incredible value.

The only downside is that the pull-aside access works better for smaller chests. If you're well-endowed, you might find the neckline doesn't provide enough coverage or support. But for me (C cup), it worked perfectly.

Kindred Bravely Sublime Nursing Bra ($45, 4.5 stars, 8K reviews) — The Best Bra For Your Money

If there's one piece of nursing gear I would tell every pregnant woman to buy before she gives birth, it's the Kindred Bravely Sublime Nursing Bra. Hands down, no contest, the most comfortable bra I have ever worn in my entire life.

And I have worn some comfortable bras. I am a connoisseur of comfortable bras. I have spent hundreds of dollars on "the most comfortable bra ever" from multiple brands. The Kindred Bravely beats them all.

The fabric is this buttery-soft bamboo-spandex blend that feels like wearing a cloud. The straps are wide enough not to dig into your shoulders but not so wide that they look like a sports bra strap. The clips are easy to open and close with one hand (critical for holding a baby with the other hand). And the fit is designed for the postpartum body, which means it accommodates the fluctuating cup sizes that happen throughout the day as you nurse and your milk comes and goes.

I bought two of these and wore them on rotation for eight months. They didn't stretch out, the clips didn't break, and the fabric didn't pill. At $45 each, they're not cheap for a bra, but they're not outrageously expensive either. And honestly, considering I wore each one roughly 150 times before I stopped nursing, the cost per wear is pennies.

The only complaint I've heard from friends is that the sizing can be tricky. Kindred Bravely has a detailed size chart, and I'd recommend actually measuring yourself instead of guessing. I'm normally a medium in tops and the medium fit perfectly, but I know friends who needed to size up or down depending on their band size and cup volume.

Ekouaer Nursing Top ($18, 4.4 stars, 9K reviews) — Fine, But Not Great

The Ekouaer nursing top is the definition of "you get what you pay for." At $18, it's cheap, it does the job, but it's not going to blow your mind.

The top has a double-layer design with a hidden slit for nursing access. The concept is solid: you lift the outer layer, access the inner layer's slit, and nurse without exposing your whole torso. In practice, it works fine. The fabric is soft and comfortable, and the fit is relaxed enough to be flattering during the early postpartum days when you don't want anything tight against your body.

But here's the problem: after about four washes, the fabric started pilling. Not dramatically, but noticeably. The black one developed little fuzz balls on the stomach area where my hand would rest while I held the baby. The hemlines started looking a little tired. By month three of regular wear, the top looked like it had been through a war.

I still wore it. It still functioned as a nursing top. But it looked like an $18 top, and if you care about your clothes looking nice after multiple washes, you might want to spend a little more.

That said, for $18, it's a perfectly adequate backup top. I kept mine in the diaper bag as an emergency change, and it served that purpose well. If it got stained or ruined, I wouldn't have cried over it.

Latched Mama Pullover ($55, 4.6 stars, 3K reviews) — The Fancy Option

Latched Mama is the brand that kept popping up in my Instagram ads. The influencers love it. The aesthetic is "cozy, rustic, farmhouse chic." The prices are boutique-level: $55 for a single pullover top.

I caved and bought one because I saw a friend wearing it and she looked adorable. And I have to admit: the quality is excellent. The fabric is thick, high-quality cotton that feels substantial. The nursing access is cleverly designed through a side slit that's almost invisible when not in use. The cut is flattering in that "effortlessly put together" way that actually requires quite a lot of effort to achieve.

But here's the thing: is it three times better than the $14 Bearsland dress? No. Absolutely not. It's better, sure. The fabric is nicer, the construction is more thought-out, the overall look is more polished. But three times better? Four times better? Not even close.

If you have the budget and you want to feel really cute and put-together while nursing, Latched Mama is great. If you're on a budget and you just need something that works, you're paying a huge premium for marginal improvement.

The Hack That Changed Everything

After months of testing all these products, here's my actual daily uniform:

  • Cheap Amazon nursing tanks — the ones that are basically a shelf bra attached to a stretchy tank top. They cost about $12 each on Amazon. I wore one under everything.
  • Regular button-down shirts — I raided my husband's closet for his old flannel shirts and button-downs. Unbutton a few buttons, pull the nursing tank down, nurse. The shirt covers everything you don't want exposed. It looks intentional. It's effortless.
  • One Kindred Bravely Sublime bra on, one in the wash. This is non-negotiable.
  • The Bearsland dresses for days when I wanted to look like I made an effort.

That combo cost me about $100 total and got me through eight months of nursing with my dignity (mostly) intact. The expensive brands are nice, but they're not necessary. The cheap Amazon stuff is genuinely good. And the nursing tank + button-down shirt hack is the real pro tip that nobody tells you about.

Where to buy: The Bearsland Nursing Dresses 3-Pack is $42 on Amazon. The Kindred Bravely Sublime Bra is $45. The Ekouaer Nursing Top is $18. The Latched Mama Pullover is $55 at latchedmama.com.
