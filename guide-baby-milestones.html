The Products That Helped My Baby Hit Her Milestones (According To Our Physical Therapist)

At six months, our daughter still wasn't rolling. A physical therapist fixed that — and pointed us to the right gear.

Full disclosure: this post contains affiliate links; if you buy through them we may earn a small commission at no extra cost. We only recommend what we genuinely used.

At her six-month checkup, our pediatrician mentioned, casually, that our daughter hadn't rolled over yet. "Let's give her another month." I spent that month in a spiral, because I had a mom-group app and 4,000 opinions. The pediatrician referred us to a physical therapist — and everything got better. Our PT never once said "she's behind." She said "let's give her more chances to practice," then walked through our house pointing at what to buy and what to donate. These are the products she actually recommended — and why.

The Lovevery Play Gym — the "toy that's a tool"

The PT's first move was to look at our play gym — a bright plastic thing from a garage sale — and say, "this is a toy, not a tool." She recommended the Lovevery Play Gym, and $140 felt like a lot for a wooden frame with fabric. But it has five developmental zones at different heights, a mirror at tummy-time level, and toys designed for each stage — which meant she always had something worth reaching for, and tummy time stopped being a crying contest. It's still in use at 15 months, now as a fort.

The Upseat — the seat our PT actually approved

I asked about the popular bucket-style seat every registry pushed, and the PT made a face like I'd offered her a lemon. Those seats hold a baby upright before they're ready and put hips in a position that works against development. She showed us the Upseat instead — developed with physical therapists, it keeps the hips neutral and lets a baby practice sitting without being propped into a posture they can't hold. We started with five-minute sessions while I made dinner; within three weeks she was sitting hands-free, reaching for the tray. If you buy one seat-related thing, buy this one.

The foam play mat — the floor the PT fixed

The second thing she fixed was our floor. Our living room is hardwood, which sounds lovely and is actually a baby's skin crime scene. The PT's exact words: "She needs a place to fall that doesn't hurt, or she'll stop trying." We bought a 16-tile foam play mat for about $35 — thicker is better — and she did tummy time without crying for the first time in her life, because the floor stopped being mean. If you have hard floors, this is not a nicety. It's the practice field.

The Oball rattle — the $5 breakthrough

For reaching and grasping work, the PT pulled an Oball rattle out of her own bag — the classic one with holes all over it. "The holes mean she can hook a finger through and catch it, even before her hands work properly," she said. It was the first toy she intentionally reached for, grabbed, and waved around like a tiny prize. She rolled over the day after she started playing with it — I'm 90 percent sure the toy is why. And here's the review that matters: the physical therapist kept it for her own kids. The best five dollars in the entire nursery.

The crawling tunnel — the "if you buy one toy" recommendation

Once sitting was sorted, the PT gave us the next assignment: "If you buy one toy for the next stage, buy a crawl tunnel." We put it at the end of the play mat, and I sat at the far end with a bowl of Cheerios: "Come get it!" She army-crawled through it at nine months and spent the next month treating it like a subway line — building core strength without realizing she was doing PT. Bonus: the dog uses it too, which the baby finds hilarious.

What I actually learned

She wasn't delayed. She was a baby on her own timeline, and we'd accidentally built her a house with no reason to move. Every product on this list did one job — it made practice possible, comfortable, and fun. The rolling happened in her sleep one night, with no audience. I cried. The dog looked confused. If your baby is "behind," get the evaluation, not the worry — and give them a floor they can conquer.

🛒 Where to buy: the Lovevery Play Gym ($140), the Upseat floor seat ($89), a thick foam play mat ($35), the Oball rattle ($5), and a pop-up crawling tunnel ($20).
