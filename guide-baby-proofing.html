Baby-Proofing Our House Took An Entire Weekend. Here's What We Actually Needed

My husband spent a weekend baby-proofing. Our crawling baby found everything he missed within 24 hours.

The weekend our daughter figured out crawling, my husband announced he was "taking care of the baby-proofing." He printed a checklist, went to the hardware store, and spent the whole weekend on his hands and knees installing things while I watched from the couch with the baby and a bag of chips. I was skeptical. He was proud.

Then our newly mobile daughter had 24 hours to test his work, and she failed us. She found the cabinet under the sink (latched, good), the toilet (locked, good), and a bookshelf in our bedroom that my husband had genuinely missed because she was absolutely not supposed to be in that room. The baby-proofing weekend taught us one lesson: the baby finds everything. Here's what actually mattered, and what was overkill.

What Actually Made It Into Our House

Safety 1st Cabinet Locks ($8 for 12) — Adhesive and adjustable, so no drilling into our rental cabinets. Installation took three seconds each, and they've survived two years of toddler yanking. We used them on every cabinet under the sink, the dishwasher, and the bathroom vanity.

Regalo Baby Gate ($39) — A pressure-mounted gate that installs in minutes and doesn't damage door frames. It's blocked the top of our stairs for two years and survived a toddler who throws himself at it daily. Measure your opening first — ours came with extensions that made it fit our wide hallway.

Outlet Covers ($5) — The cheapest item on this list and the one you genuinely can't skip. We covered every outlet below four feet in about ten minutes. The simple plug-style covers are fine; just get enough that you're not rationing them.

Corner Guards ($10) — The coffee table corner met her eyebrow exactly once. One trip to urgent care later, every sharp corner in our apartment had a guard on it. Ten dollars would have saved us a 3 p.m. urgent care visit and a very judgmental look from a nurse.

Toilet Locks ($6) — I will not be elaborating on how I know these are necessary. They click into the lid, install in thirty seconds, and cost six dollars. Just buy them before the baby learns how toilets work.

Furniture Anchors ($14) — This is the one we feel most strongly about. Kids die from furniture tip-overs every year — dressers, TVs, bookshelves. Anchoring the dresser and the TV stand took us twenty minutes and a drill. There is no version of this that isn't worth doing, and it's the first thing we tell any new parent.

Why We Recommend These

This list is our actual weekend, minus the Home Depot detour and one argument about whether the baby could open the refrigerator (she can't — we anchored it anyway). Every item is still installed in our home two years later, which is the real test: baby-proofing gear only works if you'll actually live with it.

What to Look For

  • No-drill options for rentals. Pressure mounts and adhesive saved our security deposit.
  • Gate specs. Measure the opening first, and check the height — at least 30 inches for climbers.
  • Anchor everything over two feet. Dressers, shelves, TVs. If it can tip, anchor it.
  • Install now, not later. The baby finds everything within 24 hours. We learned that the hard way.

🛒 Where to buy: All products available on Amazon, and most are under $15. Grab the full set in one order and install it before the baby starts crawling — not after.
