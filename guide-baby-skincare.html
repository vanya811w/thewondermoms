My Baby's Eczema Journey: The Products That Finally Cleared Her Skin

Six months of red, angry, bleeding patches — two doctors, one very patient mom, and the routine that finally worked.

At four months old, my daughter's cheeks turned red. Just red, at first — I assumed drool rash, teething, whatever. But by five months her elbows were cracked and weeping, she was scratching herself bloody in her sleep, and I was washing her sheets every morning because they were covered in little rust-colored flakes. I'm not going to sugarcoat this: eczema was the hardest thing we dealt with in her first year. Harder than the sleep regression. Harder than the colic.

We saw our pediatrician three times and a pediatric dermatologist once. We did the steroid course — it worked, but it came back the week we stopped. That's when the dermatologist said the thing that changed everything: "Eczema is a moisture problem, not a medicine problem. Your job is to build her barrier and hold it." We built a routine. Six weeks later, her skin was clear for the first time since it started. Here's what's in it — and I'll tell you right now, none of it is a miracle, but all of it together is.

Aquaphor Baby — The Barrier, Always

This is the foundation, the thing I never skip. Aquaphor baby healing ointment ($11) is thick petroleum jelly with a few extra soothing ingredients, and it was the only thing that held moisture in long enough to matter. At night, I slathered her hands in it and put her in a sleep sack with the cuffs pulled over her fingers so she couldn't scratch. It's greasy, it stains pajamas, and I will buy it by the gallon for the rest of my life. Our dermatologist called it "boring and essential," and honestly, that's the highest compliment a product can get.

Tubby Todd All-Over Ointment — The One That Finally Stuck

I resisted this for months because every Instagram mom was pushing it and I'm a skeptic. Then a friend's baby cleared up, and at my wit's end I ordered the Tubby Todd all-over ointment ($18). It's a balm you warm between your fingers and press into the skin — no rubbing, which matters when the skin is angry. It has that calming, powdery smell, and it was the first thing that visibly calmed her patches within two days. My skepticism did not survive contact with a working product. I now own it in two sizes, including the tiny one for the diaper bag.

Mustela Stelatopia — The Cream That Bought Us Mornings

The Mustela Stelatopia emollient cream ($27) is our every-morning and every-evening moisturizer. It's rich without being gross, and the dermatologist approved the ingredient list, which matters to me now in a way it didn't before. A pea-sized amount per cheek, morning and night, every single day — and I mean every single day, because eczema doesn't care that you were up until 1 AM.

Aveeno Baby Eczema Therapy — The Bath Rescue

Baths were a battle, because water dries skin out further. The fix was the Aveeno Baby eczema therapy soothing bath treatment ($12) — a colloidal oatmeal powder that turns the bath milky. Fifteen minutes in it, then pat dry (never rub) and moisturize within three minutes. It became the ritual that calms her down for bed, and her redness always dropped visibly by morning. Our house has one running rule now: no baths without the bag of oatmeal.

CeraVe Baby — The Lightweight Layer

On hot days, or when she needs a lighter hit mid-afternoon, we use CeraVe baby moisturizing cream ($11). It has ceramides — the same stuff that's supposed to hold skin cells together — and it sinks in fast, so it's the one I use when she's already dressed and wiggly and I just need something on her arms in thirty seconds. It layers fine over the heavier stuff, too, which makes it perfect for the "oh no, I forgot her morning lotion and we're already in the car" moments. Which happen. They happen a lot.

The Routine, In Order

Here's the whole thing, so you don't have to spend six months figuring it out: bath with oatmeal every other day (plain warm water in between), pat dry, moisturize within three minutes with Mustela, then Aquaphor on the worst spots, then the Tubby Todd pressed over everything, then mittens and a sleep sack. Morning: Mustela, CeraVe if we're going out, and that's it.

Her skin is clear now — not perfect, she'll always be prone to it, but clear. And the look on her face when she finally stopped scratching herself awake? That's the whole story. It took six months and about eighty dollars of product, and I'd do it all again. I'd do twice that, honestly.

🛒 Where to buy: All five are on Amazon: Aquaphor baby ($11), Tubby Todd all-over ointment ($18), Mustela Stelatopia ($27), the Aveeno Baby eczema bath treatment ($12), and CeraVe baby moisturizer ($11).
