Baby Meal Prep Sundays: How I Make A Week's Worth Of Baby Food In 2 Hours

A working mom's Sunday system for a week of homemade baby food — without living in the kitchen.

Every Sunday at 9 AM, my husband takes our toddler to the park, I pour a giant coffee, and I turn our kitchen into a baby food factory. Two hours later — I mean it, about two hours — there's a week's worth of food in the freezer, the kitchen's clean, and I've managed to watch exactly one episode of something in between steam cycles. I'm a working mom, so the weekday version of me cannot cook a single thing from scratch, and the 6:30 PM version of me absolutely will not. Sunday me has to handle it. Here's the gear that makes the whole operation possible.

The Baby Brezza That Ended Bottle Math

Our daughter is formula-fed, and I will die on the hill that the Baby Brezza formula maker ($199) is worth every cent. It mixes formula to the exact ratio, warm, in about three seconds — one button, no scoop, no foam, no 3 AM scoop-counting. My husband, a man who "doesn't buy gadgets," used it once and immediately apologized for questioning the purchase. Yes, you have to disassemble the funnel for cleaning every few days, and yes, I've screamed at the funnel. But the time it saves — roughly four hundred bottles' worth by my count — is ridiculous.

The Beaba Babycook — Steam + Blend in One Bowl

This is the heart of Sunday prep. The Beaba Babycook ($129) steams vegetables in one bowl and then blends them in the same bowl — you never dirty a second dish, which is the real genius. Chop sweet potato, hit steam, wait nine minutes, flip it over, blend. I do two batches of vegetables and one of fruit each Sunday, and that's basically half the week's food handled. It's the one countertop appliance I will never loan to anyone, because I know I'll never get it back.

The Freezer Tray That Makes It a System

The OXO Tot freezer tray ($10) is the unsung hero. One batch fills the tray, each cube is exactly one ounce, and they pop out like ice cubes into a labeled freezer bag. I label every bag with the date and what's inside, because I have absolutely frozen mystery purees before, and I do not recommend feeding your baby "orange stuff, probably squash?" at 5:45 PM on a Tuesday. Ten dollars, and it's the reason a two-hour prep actually feeds us all week instead of just filling the fridge with a single nice lunch.

Spoons That Survive the Toddler

The Munchkin soft-tip spoons ($5 for a six-pack) are the ones I've bought three times, because they're the only spoons our daughter will accept and because they go through the dishwasher like little orange soldiers. Soft tip for gums, angled head for tiny mouths, and six of them means we always have one even when three are hiding in the playroom. If you're registering for a baby shower, register for three packs. You'll understand later.

Bumkins Bibs — Wipeable Armor

Real talk: I own exactly one fabric bib, and it's for photos. Everything else is Bumkins waterproof bibs ($8 for three) — wipeable, with a pocket that catches ninety percent of the sweet potato casualties before they hit the carpet. At the end of a meal I wipe one with a paper towel and hang it to dry. Three bibs genuinely covers a week because they never need to go near the washing machine. My laundry pile quietly thanks them.

The Formula Pitcher for Daycare Day

Our daycare requires formula pre-made in bottles, so the Dr. Brown's formula pitcher ($12) is a Sunday-night staple. I mix the whole day's formula in one batch — zero clumps, zero bubbles, no scooping twenty-two times in a row. Pour it into bottles, fridge it, done. It also made the midnight bottles easier back in the newborn days: no waking up enough to count scoops at 2 AM. I've used it for a year and a half and it still has the exact same seal.

My Actual Two-Hour Timeline

Here's the schedule, in case it helps: 9:00 — start sweet potato and carrot batches in the Babycook. 9:12 — blend and fill the OXO tray. 9:20 — steam pears while I portion the formula pitcher. 9:35 — blend, fill tray number two, load the dishwasher. 9:45 — strip and re-clean the kitchen so I'm not looking at it all week. 10:00 — coffee on the porch, freezer full, week handled.

It's not glamorous, but neither is feeding a baby. Two hours on Sunday beats thirty minutes every single weeknight — every single time.

🛒 Where to buy: The whole Sunday operation is on Amazon: the Baby Brezza formula maker ($199), Beaba Babycook ($129), OXO Tot freezer tray ($10), Munchkin spoons ($5), Bumkins bibs ($8), and the Dr. Brown's formula pitcher ($12).
