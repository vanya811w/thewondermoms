How We Did The First Year Of Baby On A $500 Budget

We were both in grad school when I got pregnant. Our baby budget was $500 total. Here's what we bought and what we skipped.

When that second line appeared, I felt joy and then immediate panic. We were both PhD students with a combined annual income of $38,000. A baby can cost $12,000+ the first year according to every article I read. I spent weeks convinced we couldn't afford this child.

Spoiler: we could. She's 18 months old now, healthy and happy, and we spent approximately $480 on baby gear in her first year. Here's exactly how we did it — what we bought new, what we got used, and what we skipped entirely.

The Rules We Followed

Rule 1: If it can be washed, buy it used. Rule 2: If it touches food or has safety implications, buy it new. Rule 3: If you're not sure you need it, wait a week after the baby arrives to decide.

What We Bought New

IKEA Sniglar crib + mattress ($99 total): The cheapest safe sleep surface on the market. It's unfinished solid beech wood — no off-gassing, no toxic finishes. Does it look like a designer nursery? No. Does a baby care? Also no. At $79 for the crib and $20 for the mattress, it was our biggest single purchase and 100% worth it.

Infantino Flip carrier ($29): Not the fanciest, but it's safety-certified and held our daughter through months 2-8. The padding is minimal, so it's only comfortable for about 30 minutes at a time. For a trip to the grocery store or a walk around the block, it was perfectly adequate. We upgraded to a used Ergobaby ($45 on Facebook Marketplace) around 8 months when she got heavier.

Dr. Brown's formula pitcher ($12): Instead of a $199 formula maker, we used this $12 pitcher to mix a day's worth of formula each morning. It took 2 minutes. The $187 we saved paid for 3 months of formula.

SwaddleMe swaddles ($18 for 3-pack): The cheapest pocket swaddle that actually contains a newborn's arms. We used these for about 8 weeks until she started rolling, then transitioned to a used Halo SleepSack we found for $8 at Once Upon A Child.

What We Bought Used

Facebook Marketplace was our best friend. We got a baby bathtub for $5, a Boppy nursing pillow for $8, a bouncer for $15, and bags of clothing for about $40 total (babies wear clothes for approximately 8 minutes before outgrowing them, so used clothes are basically new). We also got an IKEA Antilop high chair for $15 — it's $25 new, but someone had already assembled it. Their loss, our gain.

What We Skipped

We did not buy: a changing table (used a $10 changing pad on the floor), a diaper pail (used a regular trash can with a lid), a bottle warmer (our baby drank cold formula and never knew the difference), a wipe warmer (dries out wipes and is completely unnecessary), or a baby monitor (our apartment was 700 square feet — we could hear her from every room).

The biggest lesson: your baby doesn't need most of what the baby industry tells you they need. They need a safe place to sleep, a way to eat, something to wear, a car seat, and you. Everything else is negotiable.

🛒 Where to buy: IKEA Sniglar (IKEA only), Infantino carrier, Dr. Brown's pitcher, SwaddleMe swaddles — available on Amazon. Check Facebook Marketplace for everything else.
