How We Did The First Year Of Baby On A $500 Budget (The Cheapest Versions Of Everything That Actually Work)

Grad student parents, a teaching-assistant salary, and a registry that made us want to cry. Here's what we actually spent money on.

Full disclosure: this post contains affiliate links; if you buy through them we may earn a small commission at no extra cost. We only recommend what we genuinely used.

We had our daughter in the middle of my husband's master's program. I was a teaching assistant. Our combined "fun money" was a negative number. When the registry apps started recommending $1,400 strollers and $300 bassinets, I nearly had a panic attack in Target. So we made a spreadsheet and sorted every purchase into three columns: buy new, buy used, skip entirely. The big stuff came in around $500, and everything on this list earned its place.

New: the IKEA Sniglar crib — $99 of no-regrets

The crib was our one big new purchase, and we bought the cheapest safe one that exists: the IKEA Sniglar. About $99, solid wood, no drop sides, meets every safety standard. We skipped the bassinet entirely — a bassinet is a crib that costs more and lasts less. The mattress was our one splurge-ish item: a firm, basic, safe crib mattress bought on sale. The whole sleep setup cost less than one fancy bassinet, and it'll be her bed until she's two.

New: the Infantino carrier — $29 of pure gold

Everyone told us to buy the $150 ergonomic carrier. We bought the Infantino Flip 4-in-1 for $29 instead — it does four positions, holds 8 to 32 pounds, and survives the washing machine. I wore her through a full semester of library study sessions and hundreds of trips to the kitchen, and it never once let go. Buy carriers new — you don't know what a used strap has been through.

New: cloth diapers — $42 that shocked us

Cloth was our "this is going to be gross" experiment that wasn't. We bought the ALVABABY one-size pocket diapers — six diapers with twelve inserts for about $42 — and they adjust to fit from 8 pounds to toddler size, so the same six lasted the whole first year. We did cloth at home, disposables on outings, which cut our disposable bill roughly in half. The washing isn't gross, I promise. The math: disposables run $70 to $90 a month; cloth was $42 once, plus a dollar a week in detergent.

New: SwaddleMe swaddles — the $18 sleep economy

The SwaddleMe Original three-pack is about $18, and it was the difference between sleeping and not sleeping. The wings wrap and Velcro shut in ten seconds, and our daughter slept six-hour stretches by week eight in these. They're the only "you'll buy it anyway" item on this list, so buy the cheap one.

New: the Dr. Brown's formula pitcher — the best $12 in baby

We formula-fed, because grad school waits for no one. The Dr. Brown's mixing pitcher costs $12 and made formula feel like a luxury. You mix a full day at once — the little blade dissolves everything, no clumps, no foam — then pour as needed. The 2 a.m. feed became: open fridge, pour, warm, done. It paid for itself in the first week, in sleep and in not-spilled scoops of powder.

New: the IKEA Antilop high chair — $25, forever

The IKEA Antilop high chair, about $25, no cushions, no covers, no nonsense. When our daughter threw yogurt at it, one wet paper towel fixed it. The $300 high chairs have covers you have to disassemble with an Allen wrench to wash. The tray pops off, the whole thing rinses, and it will outlive the baby.

Used, skipped, and the truth

Used: the stroller ($60 off Facebook Marketplace), a bouncer (free from our Buy Nothing group), and every book she owns. Skipped: the wipe warmer, the bottle sterilizer (a pot of boiling water does the same job), the changing table (a dresser with a $10 mat is the same thing), and the nursery theme. The registry pressure is a trap. Your baby needs: a safe place to sleep, a way to eat, a way to be carried, and a few things to look at. That's the whole list. Everything else is marketing.
