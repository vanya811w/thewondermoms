How I Set Up Our Nursery For Under $500 (And What Was Actually Worth Splurging On)

We moved into a two-bedroom at 32 weeks pregnant. Here's our full nursery budget — and the one thing we're glad we splurged on.

At 32 weeks pregnant, our landlord decided to sell the house we were renting. We packed up, moved into a two-bedroom apartment with a "nursery" the size of a walk-in closet, and suddenly the nursery was a budget spreadsheet and a floor plan I drew on a napkin. I did the IKEA trip at 35 weeks, sitting on chairs in the showroom because standing was no longer an option.

Here's the thing nobody tells you: nurseries don't need to cost $3,000. Ours came in right around $400, everything included, and it did the job beautifully. The secret was spending big where it mattered and going cheap everywhere else. Add it up as you read — 99 + 59 + 69 + 25 + 129, plus a few odds and ends we already owned. We stayed under budget, and the baby didn't care about any of it. Here's exactly what we bought.

What We Bought (And What I'd Do Differently)

IKEA Sniglar Crib ($99) — The most honest piece of baby furniture on earth: it's wood, it's a crib, it holds a baby. IKEA cribs meet the same safety standards as the $800 ones, and this one survived a toddler who treated the rails like a jungle gym. It's from IKEA, not Amazon — still the best $99 we spent.

Newton Waterproof Mattress Cover ($59) — By week six we'd had two blowouts, one pee fountain, and zero stains. Waterproof, wipeable, and it doesn't crinkle like the cheap plastic ones. A waterproof layer is non-negotiable — I cannot say this loudly enough. Do not skip it.

Hatch Sound Machine ($69) — The one "splurge" in our cheap categories, and worth every dollar. White noise, a dimmable nightlight, and a schedule I set from my phone, so baby hears the same sound at our house and grandma's. When it broke a year later, we replaced it within the week without discussion.

Blackout Curtains ($25) — Two cheap panels of blackout curtains turned our bright little room into a cave, and the difference in naps was immediate and dramatic. The 4 p.m. nap became a real thing. Twenty-five dollars changed our lives more than anything else on this list.

Keekaroo Peanut Changer ($129) — This was our actual splurge, and I'd do it again tomorrow. It's a wipeable changing pad you clean with a single wipe instead of washing covers at 3 a.m. We've done roughly 2,000 diaper changes on ours and it looks brand new. The $20 pad-and-cover combos? You will hate them within a week.

Why We Recommend These

This list was built from one real nursery, on a real budget, with a baby who arrived two weeks early and a landlord who gave us zero warning. Every purchase survived actual use. Our rule of thumb: splurge on the things you touch daily — the changing pad, the mattress protection, the sound machine — and save on everything a baby won't remember anyway.

What to Look For

  • Safety standards. For cribs, look for JPMA certification and no drop-side rails, ever.
  • Small footprint. In a tiny room, vertical storage and wall-mounted everything win.
  • Wipeable surfaces. If it can't be cleaned with one wipe at 3 a.m., it's not nursery material.
  • Durability over trends. Buy the crib that survives toddler number two, not the one that matches a Pinterest mood board.

🛒 Where to buy: The Sniglar crib is from IKEA (around $99 in store and online); everything else is on Amazon. Prices change constantly — blackout curtains especially — so check current deals before you buy.
