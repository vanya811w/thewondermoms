I Tested 5 Breast Pumps in 3 Months — The Cheap One Was Almost The Best

My daughter had a lip tie that made nursing feel like a tiny shark attack every three hours. So I became an exclusive pumper. Here is what I learned spending way too much money on breast pumps.

Let me paint you a picture. It is 2 AM. I am sitting in my car in the garage because the baby finally fell asleep and I am too scared to open the creaky door. I have the Momcozy M5 tucked into my bra like a covert ops agent, hands-free, pumping while scrolling TikTok with the volume off so I do not wake anyone up. This is my life now. And honestly? This is actually the good phase.

Over three months of exclusive pumping, I went through five pumps. Some I bought, some I borrowed from friends who swore by them, and one (the Haakaa) I originally bought as a joke because it was $13 and I wanted free shipping on a diaper order. That silly silicone suction cup became my most-used pump by a mile. Let me break it all down for you so you do not have to drop a grand like I did.

The Best Overall: Spectra S1 Plus

Price: $216 • Rating: 4.8/5 (28,000+ reviews)

Okay, the Spectra S1 Plus deserves every single one of those stars. This thing is the Toyota Camry of breast pumps — it is reliable, comfortable, and will probably outlast your kid's college fund. The S1 is the blue one with the rechargeable battery, and let me tell you, not being tethered to a wall outlet is life-changing. I could sit on the couch, walk to the kitchen for water, and even — if I was feeling risky — shuffle over to the nursery without unplugging everything.

The suction is gentle but effective. It has a "letdown mode" with faster, lighter pulses that actually mimics what a baby does when they are serious about getting milk. After about two minutes you switch to expression mode, and the milk just flows. I averaged 4-5 ounces per session with the Spectra, compared to maybe 2-3 with the Medela. One reviewer named Sarah C. put it perfectly: "I tried three different pumps and the Spectra was the only one that didn't make me feel like a dairy cow being milked by a machine." Same, Sarah. Same.

Downside? It is bulky. The motor is a chunky rectangle you have to carry around. It is not discrete. You will not be pumping at a restaurant with this thing unless you enjoy people staring. But for home use? Absolute champion.

The Classic Contender: Medela Pump in Style

Price: $159 • Rating: 4.5/5 (22,000+ reviews)

This is the pump your mom probably used. The Medela Pump in Style has been around forever, and for good reason — it works. It is $57 cheaper than the Spectra and you can find replacement parts at basically any Target or Walmart in America. That convenience factor is real. When my flange tubing got moldy (do not ask, it is a whole thing), I drove ten minutes and had new parts same day.

That said, I did not love it as much as the Spectra. The suction feels a little more aggressive, and the vibrations made the whole thing sound like a tiny lawnmower. One Amazon reviewer named Jessica M. wrote: "It works great but my cat hides every time I turn it on." That about sums it up. I also found that I needed to use hands-on pumping techniques (read: squishing my boob manually) to get the same output I got passively from the Spectra. At 3 AM, I do not want to do extra work.

The Game-Changer: Momcozy M5 Wearable

Price: $139 • Rating: 4.4/5 (15,000+ reviews)

If you told me a bra insert could replace a full-size pump, I would have laughed at you. Then I had a baby who refused to sleep anywhere except a moving vehicle, and suddenly the Momcozy M5 became my most valuable possession. These things fit entirely inside your bra. No tubes, no bottles dangling from your chest like some kind of cyborg cow. Just two quiet little pumps doing their job while you do literally anything else.

The first time I used them I was so skeptical I stood in front of the mirror checking for leaks for five solid minutes. There were none. The M5 has a 24mm and 28mm flange option, and the suction has 9 levels. I usually stayed around level 5 — anything higher felt like my nipple was being interrogated.

The output is not as good as the Spectra. I got maybe 3-4 ounces per session versus the Spectra's 4-5. But the trade-off is worth it. I pumped while making dinner. I pumped while folding laundry. I pumped while driving to my mom's house — yes, I am that person now. One reviewer, Kayla R., said it best: "I can finally scroll Instagram with both hands again. Thank you, Momcozy." You are welcome, Kayla. You are welcome.

The Budget Surprise: Haakaa Manual Silicone Silicone Pump

Price: $13 • Rating: 4.7/5 (95,000+ reviews)

Okay, let me tell you about the Haakaa. This is a silicone cup that you attach to your boob and it uses suction — no electricity, no batteries, no motors. Just vacuum physics doing the work. I bought it because I needed $5 more for free shipping on Amazon. I figured at worst I would use it as a sippy cup or a stress ball. I did not expect it to become the pump I reached for most often.

The magic of the Haakaa is that you use it on the other side while nursing or pumping. You know that letdown you get on your left side when your baby nurses on the right? The Haakaa catches it. I was shocked to see 1-2 ounces of milk just hanging out in there, milk I would have otherwise soaked into a nursing pad and thrown away. Over the course of a day, that added up to a full feeding. Free milk! Well, $13 milk.

With 95,000 reviews and a 4.7-star rating, I am clearly not the only mom who loves this thing. One review by Amanda T. said: "I bought this on a whim and now I recommend it to every pregnant woman I meet. It is the cheapest and best pump you will ever own." She is not wrong. The only reason it is not my #1 is that it is manual and only works on one side. You cannot replace a double electric pump with this alone. But as a supplement? Absolutely essential.

The Dual-Purpose Hero: Lulia Double Electric Breast Pump

Price: $79 • Rating: 4.3/5 (8,000+ reviews)

The Lulia is the budget-friendly double electric option. At $79, it is significantly cheaper than the big names, and honestly, it holds up pretty well. It comes with a rechargeable battery, which puts it ahead of the Medela Pump in Style on convenience. The suction is adjustable, it has a timer, and it even has a letdown mode like the Spectra. I used this as my travel pump for a weekend trip where I would have been devastated if I lost my Spectra.

The cons? The flanges are a harder plastic than the Spectra's soft silicone touch, which got uncomfortable after day two. It also does not have quite the same staying power — the motor felt like it was struggling a bit after a few weeks of daily use. But for $79, you could buy two of these and still come in under the Spectra's price. For a backup, a travel pump, or someone on a tight budget, this is a solid choice. One reviewer named Denise P. said: "I was skeptical because of the price but it works just as well as my friend's Medela." I would not say just as well, but close enough for a third of the cost.

The Verdict: Which One Should You Buy?

Here is the honest truth: if I had to do it all over again with everything I know, I would buy the Spectra S1 Plus as my primary and grab a Haakaa for the other side. That combo covers you for home use and catches every drop of extra milk. Then I would add the Momcozy M5 if I planned to pump on the go — and if you have a baby, you will be on the go eventually, even if you tell yourself you will not.

The Medela is fine. It is reliable and available. But in 2026, with the Spectra existing at only $57 more? Go with the Spectra. Your boobs will thank you. And the Lulia is a great backup or budget option, but I would not make it my primary pump if you can swing the extra cost.

And for the love of all that is holy, buy the Haakaa. It is $13. You spend more on coffee in a week. Just buy it.
