Product Review

3 Diaper Pails, 1 Week Of Real Dirty Diapers Each. Only One Didn't Make Me Gag.

I took smell-containment very seriously. My husband thought I'd lost my mind. He's not wrong, but at least our nursery doesn't smell like a landfill.

Here's something the baby books don't prepare you for: by the time your baby is eating solids, their diapers smell like something died, resurrected, and died again. The cute little newborn mustard poops? A distant memory. By month 6, you're dealing with biological warfare in a Pampers wrapper.

A good diaper pail isn't a luxury — it's a marriage saver. I tested three of the top sellers for one week each with my then-8-month-old's... contributions. Here's what I learned through my nose.

The Contenders

  • Ubbi Steel Diaper Pail — $69, powder-coated steel, uses ANY bag — 4.7★ (22,000+ reviews)
  • Diaper Genie Complete — $45, proprietary bag refills, foot pedal — 4.6★ (35,000+ reviews)
  • Munchkin Step — $65, baking soda puck system, self-sealing — 4.4★ (10,000+ reviews)

Ubbi: The Steel Fortress

The Ubbi is made of powder-coated steel — like a mini trash can crossed with a bank vault. This matters because plastic absorbs odors over time. Steel doesn't. After 6 months of use, my Ubbi still doesn't smell when empty. My sister's plastic pail (which shall remain nameless) permanently smells like the memory of diapers past.

The killer feature: it takes regular kitchen trash bags. Not proprietary $8 refill rings. Not special cartridges. Just pull a bag from the box under your sink. Over two years of diapering, this saves roughly $120-150 in refill costs. The pail basically pays for itself.

Downsides: no foot pedal. You slide open a top hatch with your hand, which means you're touching the pail with potentially dirty hands. We keep hand sanitizer next to it. Also, when you open it to take out a full bag, the odor that rushes out is... significant. Hold your breath.

Diaper Genie: The Proprietary System Tax

The Diaper Genie works differently — it's a continuous bag system where each diaper gets twisted and sealed into its own little plastic sausage link. When the pail is full, you cut the bag with a built-in cutter and tie it off. The next bag segment is already in place.

It works well. The foot pedal is satisfying. The sealing mechanism genuinely reduces odor when you open it to add a new diaper. But those refill rings. $8 each, and you'll go through about one every 3-4 weeks depending on diaper volume. That's $100-130 per year just in bags. After two kids, you could have bought three Ubbis with the refill savings.

Also: if you accidentally pull too much bag through, there's no way to push it back. You just wasted 2 feet of refill. This happened to me at least 5 times before I learned to be careful.

Munchkin Step: The Baking Soda Gimmick

The Munchkin has a built-in baking soda puck compartment that's supposed to absorb odors. It's a nice idea, but the puck needs replacing every 30 days — which is just another subscription cost ($5/month). The self-sealing mechanism is finicky and sometimes the bag snags on the internal plastic gears.

It does have a foot pedal and looks nicer than the Diaper Genie. But at $65 plus baking soda pucks, it's the most expensive to own and the least effective at smell containment. When the baking soda puck was fresh, it worked about as well as the others. When the puck got old? Noticeably worse.

The Verdict

I own the Ubbi and would buy it again. The steel construction, the ability to use any bag, and the fact that it still doesn't smell after 6 months of heavy use make it the clear winner. The lack of a foot pedal is the only real downside, and I've made peace with it.

The Diaper Genie is fine — it works, millions of parents use it, you won't hate it. But the refill cost adds up fast. If you're planning on more than one kid in diapers, do the math before buying.

The Munchkin is the one I'd skip. It costs as much as the Ubbi upfront and costs more over time with those baking soda pucks.

🛒 Where to buy: All three available on Amazon. The Ubbi occasionally drops to $55. Budget tip: generic Diaper Genie refills on Amazon cost about 40% less than the name brand.
